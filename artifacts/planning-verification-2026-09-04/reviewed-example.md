# Four-week spreadsheet basics plan: eight hours

Your budget is **2 hours × 4 weeks = 8 hours**, not twelve [E1]. This plan aims to build basic skills; it does not guarantee competence within eight hours.

Use one fictional expense sheet throughout, with Date, Item, Category, Actual, and Planned columns. Each week has two 60-minute sessions. This split is a scheduling preference, not an evidence-backed claim about retention; combine the sessions if preferred.

| Week | Session A — 60 minutes | Session B — 60 minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1: Enter and edit | 15 minutes checking application access and locating cells, rows, columns, and save controls; 35 minutes entering 15 fictional expenses; 10 minutes saving and reopening. | 15 minutes selecting and editing cells; 30 minutes formatting headers, dates, amounts, and widths; 15 minutes correcting three deliberately mistyped entries. |
| 2: Calculate | 15 minutes learning formulas and cell references; 35 minutes calculating Actual totals with SUM and averages with AVERAGE; 10 minutes checking a three-row subtotal by hand. | 15 minutes reviewing references; 30 minutes entering Planned amounts and copying an Actual minus Planned formula down the rows; 15 minutes changing inputs and checking results. |
| 3: Organize and explain | 15 minutes learning whole-table sorting and filtering; 30 minutes sorting amounts and filtering one category; 15 minutes confirming each row's values stayed together. | 15 minutes reviewing data; 30 minutes creating one chart from five items and their amounts; 15 minutes checking labels and writing two observations. |
| 4: Practice independently | 10 minutes reviewing weak spots; 40 minutes building a fresh ten-row expense sheet with formatting, a total, and copied difference formulas; 10 minutes checking calculations. | 40 minutes completing the progress check; 20 minutes correcting errors and recording remaining difficulties. |

**Progress check:** Without step-by-step notes, add two expenses, update the total's range, change an amount and verify recalculation, sort the whole table, filter one category, create a labeled chart, and save/reopen. Mark each task independent, needed help, or unfinished. Verify a small subtotal by hand and confirm row relationships after sorting. This is a diagnostic, not a pass/fail certification.

**Adjustments and tradeoffs:** Reusing one dataset prioritizes repetition over variety. Defer lookups, conditional formulas, and additional charts to protect practice time. If formulas or sorting remain difficult, replace Week 3's chart session with review and omit the chart from the final check. If access or basic typing/file operations need extra time, replace 15 minutes of Week 1 formatting with that work; if necessary, also reduce the dataset to ten rows. Longer setup delays reduce practice within the same budget. Any practice after Week 4 requires additional time.
